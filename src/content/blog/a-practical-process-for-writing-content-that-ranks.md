Plenty of businesses publish regularly and see nothing happen. The problem is rarely effort. It is usually the absence of a process that connects what you write to what people search for and what search engines reward. Here is a workflow you can repeat for every article or service page.

## Step 1: Pick a topic with a purpose

Every piece of content should have a job. Decide whether it exists to attract new visitors, convince people considering you, or capture people ready to buy. Choose a topic from your keyword research, and confirm that real people search for it and that the results are something you could realistically compete with.

## Step 2: Study the current results

Search your target phrase and read the top results carefully. Note what they cover, how they are structured, how long they are and what they miss. You are not copying them. You are finding the standard you need to meet and the gaps you can fill with better examples, clearer explanation or first-hand experience.

## Step 3: Build an outline around questions

List the questions a reader needs answered, in the order they would ask them. Use the 'People also ask' box, Search Console queries and the questions your clients raise on calls. Turn these into your headings. A strong outline makes the draft faster and keeps it focused on the reader's problem.

## Step 4: Write for a person

- Answer the main question early, then add depth.
- Use short paragraphs, plain words and concrete examples.
- Use your target phrase naturally in the title, the first part of the page and a few headings, but never force it.
- Include related terms and subtopics a knowledgeable writer would naturally cover.
- Show real experience: a client scenario, a mistake you have seen, a number from your own work.

## Step 5: Add what makes it useful

Original value is what separates content that ranks from content that merely exists. Add a checklist, a template, a worked example, a comparison table, a screenshot or a clear recommendation. Where claims need support, cite reliable sources. Make it obvious who wrote the piece and why they are qualified.

## Step 6: Optimize the page

Write a clear title tag and meta description, use one H1 and logical subheadings, compress images and add descriptive alt text. Add internal links to related pages and to the relevant service page, and link the new page from at least a few existing ones.

## Step 7: Publish, then promote

Share the piece where your audience already is, such as LinkedIn, newsletters and communities. Early engagement and links help search engines notice it sooner.

## Step 8: Review and improve

After six to eight weeks, check Search Console for the queries the page appears for. If it ranks on page two for the target phrase, strengthen the content, add depth, improve the intro and add internal links. If it ranks for an unexpected phrase, consider adjusting the page toward it.

Content that ranks is rarely a one-and-done effort. The strongest performers are usually updated over time, which keeps them accurate and signals ongoing care.

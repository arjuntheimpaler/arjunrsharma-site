Social platforms change their rules, and reach rises and falls with algorithms. An email list does not. A newsletter gives you direct, lasting access to people who chose to hear from you, and for consultants it is often the quietest and most effective way to stay top of mind until someone is ready to hire.

## Why it works

Most buyers are not ready to buy when they first encounter you. A newsletter keeps you present, builds trust through repeated useful contact and lets you share more depth than a social post allows. When a need arises, you are the person they think of.

## Decide the purpose and audience

Define who it is for and what they will get. 'Practical SEO and AI search tips for service business owners, every two weeks' is clearer than 'my thoughts on marketing'.

## Keep the format simple

- One main idea per issue, explained clearly.
- A short, practical takeaway the reader can use immediately.
- One link or resource worth sharing.
- A soft mention of how to work with you, only when relevant.

## Set a sustainable schedule

Choose a frequency you can keep for a year, whether weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Consistency matters more than volume.

## Grow the list ethically

Offer a useful lead magnet, such as a checklist or template, on your website. Invite people from LinkedIn and your talks. Collect consent clearly and comply with privacy laws that apply to you, including offering a simple way to unsubscribe.

## Choose a tool

Use any reputable email platform with good deliverability and simple tools for sign-ups and unsubscribe handling. Start with the basics and keep the design clean.

## Reuse your content

Turn newsletter issues into LinkedIn posts, blog articles and talks, and the reverse. Writing once and using it several ways keeps the workload manageable.

A testimonial reduces risk for a prospective client. 'Great to work with!' says little. 'Our qualified enquiries tripled in eight months, and for the first time I can see which marketing spend leads to revenue' says a great deal.

## Ask at the right time

Ask soon after delivering a clear result, when the client is most appreciative. Do not wait for months.

## Ask better questions

Instead of asking for a testimonial, ask three questions.

- What problem were you facing before we started?
- What was it like working together?
- What results have you seen?

Their answers can be edited lightly, with approval, into a strong quote.

## Make it credible

Include the person's name, role, company and a photo where possible. Add a link to the full case study. Video testimonials are particularly persuasive when clients are comfortable recording one.

## Place it well

Put testimonials near the moments of decision: on service pages, the contact page, proposals and your LinkedIn Featured section. Match the testimonial to the audience, using a relevant industry quote on each page.

Always have permission, and keep quotes honest. Fabricated or exaggerated testimonials damage trust and may be unlawful.

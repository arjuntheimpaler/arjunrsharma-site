Google Search Console is free, comes directly from Google and shows how your site appears in search. You do not need to live inside it. A fifteen-minute weekly routine covering the right reports is enough to catch problems early and find opportunities.

## 1. Performance: queries and pages

The Performance report shows clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position. Compare the last 28 days against the previous period. Look at which queries and pages gained or lost clicks. A sudden drop on an important page is worth investigating immediately.

## 2. Queries in positions 8 to 20

Filter queries by average position and look for phrases where you rank on the bottom of page one or on page two. These are often the fastest wins. Improving the page content, internal links and title for these queries can move them into more visible positions.

## 3. Pages indexing

The Pages report lists which URLs are indexed and why others are not. Check for new errors and watch for important pages marked as 'Crawled, currently not indexed' or 'Discovered, currently not indexed'. Unexpected spikes in excluded pages often point to a technical issue.

## 4. Core Web Vitals and Page Experience

Check whether groups of URLs have moved from 'good' to 'needs improvement'. This is useful after site updates, new plugins or redesigns.

## 5. Manual actions and security issues

These reports should be empty. If they are not, treat them as urgent.

## Make it a habit

Keep a short log of what you noticed each week and the action you took. Over a few months, that log becomes a clear record of what moved your results. Also check that your sitemap is submitted and processing without errors, and connect Search Console to your analytics tool if you want a fuller picture.

The goal is not to stare at charts. It is to spot the one or two things each week worth acting on.

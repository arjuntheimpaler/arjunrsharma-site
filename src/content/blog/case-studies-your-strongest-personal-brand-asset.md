Claims are easy to make. Case studies show the work. For a consultant, a handful of well-told client stories does more for credibility than any slogan, because they let a buyer imagine the same result in their own business.

## What a good case study contains

- The client and their context: who they are, their size and their situation.
- The problem: what was not working and what was at stake.
- The approach: what you did and why, in enough detail to show your thinking.
- The outcome: specific, honest results, with timeframes.
- The client's voice: a quote that adds human detail.

## Get permission and be accurate

Always secure the client's approval, and agree on what can be named. Where you cannot name them, describe them accurately, such as 'a regional legal consultancy'. Use real numbers and do not inflate them. Note the context that affects results, such as starting point and other factors.

## Tell it as a story

A clear arc, from problem to decision to result, is easier to read and remember than a list of deliverables. Lead with the outcome in the headline or first paragraph, then explain how you got there.

## Use it everywhere

Publish each one on your website, summarize it in a LinkedIn post, include it in proposals and pitch decks and share it with referral partners. One strong case study can be reused in many forms.

Aim to produce one new case study every quarter. Over time, they become the core of your proof.

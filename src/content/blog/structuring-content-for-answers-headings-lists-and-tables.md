Machines extract answers from the structure of a page as much as from its words. A well-organized page is easier for search engines and AI systems to understand, and easier for human readers to scan. Structure is one of the cheapest AEO improvements available.

## Headings that act as questions

Use one H1 for the page topic, then H2 and H3 headings that mirror real questions or clear topics. A heading like 'How much does a website audit cost?' tells both readers and machines exactly what the section answers. Keep the heading order logical, without skipping levels.

## Lists for steps and options

When a process has steps, use a real numbered or bulleted list instead of a dense paragraph. Start each step with a verb and keep items parallel. Lists are easy to lift into snippets and easy for people to follow.

## Tables for comparisons

When comparing options, such as plans, tools or approaches, use a proper table with labeled columns. Do not build tables out of images, since machines cannot read them.

## Short paragraphs and clear definitions

Keep paragraphs to a few sentences. Define key terms in a sentence the first time they appear, in the form 'X is Y that does Z'. Definitions are among the most frequently extracted passages.

## Consistency matters

Apply a consistent pattern across similar pages so that each one answers the same core questions in the same places. Over time, that consistency makes your site predictable and easy to interpret.

Thought leadership has a reputation for being empty. At its best, it simply means sharing useful, original thinking from real experience, so that people trust you before you ask for anything.

## Lead with usefulness

Ask whether a reader could do something better after reading your piece. If yes, you are being useful. If the only takeaway is that you are great, you are being promotional.

## Take a position

Generic advice blends into the noise. Explain what you believe, why and how it differs from common practice. You do not need to be controversial, but you do need to be specific.

## Show your work

Share how you reached conclusions: the data, the experiment, the client situation. Showing process builds credibility far more than claims of expertise.

## Be honest about limits

Say when something does not apply, when you were wrong or when the answer depends. Honesty is rare and memorable.

## Let calls to action be light

Mention how people can work with you at natural moments, such as in your profile, at the end of a case study or when someone asks. The goal is to be easy to find when someone is ready.

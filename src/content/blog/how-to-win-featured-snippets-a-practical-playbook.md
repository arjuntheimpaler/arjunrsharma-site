A featured snippet is the highlighted box at the top of some search results that shows a direct answer pulled from a web page, along with the page title and link. Winning one can put you above every other organic result and increase your visibility dramatically. Snippets change often and there is no guarantee, but a systematic approach improves your odds.

## Know the formats

Google shows snippets in several forms, and your content should match the form that fits the query.

- Paragraph snippets answer definition and explanation questions, such as 'what is technical SEO'.
- List snippets show steps or ranked items, such as 'how to set up Google Business Profile'.
- Table snippets show comparisons and data, such as pricing or specification comparisons.
- Video snippets point to a specific part of a video.

## Step 1: Find your opportunities

Start with queries you already rank for on page one, especially positions two through ten, since pages already near the top are the ones Google considers for snippets. Use Search Console to list them. Then search each query yourself to see whether a snippet appears and who owns it. If a snippet exists and is weak, outdated or incomplete, you have a chance to beat it.

## Step 2: Match the question

Use the question as a heading, written the way people ask it. Directly beneath it, answer the question in a short, self-contained block. For paragraph snippets, roughly 40 to 60 words is a useful target, though it is a guideline, not a rule. State the answer plainly, using the key terms from the question.

## Step 3: Format for the snippet type

- For lists, use real HTML ordered or unordered lists with short, parallel items.
- For tables, use real HTML tables with clear headers.
- For steps, begin each step with an action verb.

## Step 4: Add depth below the answer

The snippet answer is the hook. Below it, add explanation, examples, caveats and next steps. This gives the reader reasons to click through and gives search engines confidence that the page is a thorough resource.

## Step 5: Support the page

Make sure the page is fast, mobile friendly, clearly structured and linked to from related pages on your site. Keep it accurate and up to date.

## Step 6: Monitor and refine

Check whether you win or lose snippets using a rank tracking tool that reports snippet ownership, or check manually for key queries. If a competitor holds the snippet, compare their format and clarity with yours and improve yours.

## A word on clicks

Snippets can reduce clicks for simple factual questions, since the searcher gets the answer. Target queries where the full answer needs more than a sentence, or where the snippet naturally leads to a decision, such as how-to, cost and comparison questions. Those drive both visibility and qualified visits.

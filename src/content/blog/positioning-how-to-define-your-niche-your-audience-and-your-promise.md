Positioning is the single most important decision in personal branding, and the one most people avoid. A vague position, such as 'I help businesses grow', is easy to say and impossible to remember. A clear one, such as 'I help dental clinics get more local patients through search', is memorable, easy to refer and easy to trust.

## Why specificity works

Buyers prefer specialists for important problems. They assume a specialist has seen their situation before. Specificity also makes marketing easier, because you know exactly whom you are talking to and what they care about. And it makes referrals easier, since people can describe you in a sentence.

## Step 1: List your strengths

Write down the work you do best, the problems you solve repeatedly and the results you have delivered. Include the work that clients praise and that you enjoy.

## Step 2: Look at your clients

Review your past and current clients. Which were the most profitable, most satisfying and best at referring others? Look for patterns in industry, size, stage and problem. Your best-fit clients show you your natural niche.

## Step 3: Check the market

Does a meaningful number of people need this? Will they pay for it? Who else serves them, and how are they positioned? You are looking for a space where demand is real and where you can be distinctly better or different.

## Step 4: Write a positioning statement

Use this structure as a draft: 'I help [specific audience] achieve [specific outcome] through [your approach], so they can [benefit].' For example: 'I help service businesses build predictable lead flow through websites, SEO and AI search optimization.' Refine it until it is clear, specific and true.

## Step 5: Choose your differentiators

Decide what makes your approach distinct. It could be your process, your experience, your values or your style of working. Pick two or three you can demonstrate, not just claim.

## Step 6: Test it

Say it aloud in conversations and on calls. Notice reactions. Do people ask relevant follow-up questions? Do they say 'that is exactly what I need'? Adjust based on what you learn.

## Do not fear narrowing

Many people worry that a niche will turn away work. In practice, a clear position tends to attract better work, and you can still take adjacent projects. You can also widen the niche as your reputation grows.

## Revisit it yearly

Markets change and so do you. Review your positioning once a year, and update it deliberately.

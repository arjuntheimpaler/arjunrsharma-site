Most business writing builds up to the point. Answer-first writing does the opposite: it states the answer immediately, then adds context. This style suits readers who are skimming and suits machines that extract passages, which is why it is the backbone of AEO.

## The pattern

Under each question-style heading, write one or two sentences that fully answer the question on their own. Then expand with detail, examples and nuance. If someone read only that first block, they should come away with the correct answer.

## An example

Weak: 'Many factors can influence how much SEO costs, and every business is different, so it is hard to say.' Better: 'SEO for a small service business typically costs a monthly retainer, with the price depending on competition, site size and how much content is needed. The sections below explain what drives the price.'

## Rules of thumb

- Restate the key terms from the question in the answer.
- Keep the opening answer to about 40 to 60 words.
- Avoid pronouns like 'it' or 'this' at the start, so the passage makes sense out of context.
- Be specific. Include a range, a definition or a clear yes or no where possible.
- Put nuance after the answer, not before it.

## Where to apply it

Use it for definitions, costs, timelines, comparisons and process questions on your service pages and articles. Start by rewriting the first paragraph under every heading on your top five pages, and you will see the difference in clarity right away.

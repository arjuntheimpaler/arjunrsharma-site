Structured data is code that labels what your content means. It is often described as an AEO magic bullet. It is not, but it does help machines interpret your pages precisely, and that has real value.

## What it does and does not do

Structured data helps search engines understand the entities and facts on your page, and it can make pages eligible for specific search features. It does not guarantee a featured snippet, does not replace good content and is ignored if it does not match what is visible on the page.

## Worth implementing

- Organization or LocalBusiness: identifies your business, contact details, hours and profiles. This is the most important one for service businesses.
- Person: for authors and founders, with credentials and profile links.
- Article or BlogPosting: connects content to its author, date and publisher.
- Service: describes what you offer and where.
- BreadcrumbList: clarifies site structure.
- FAQPage: marks up question and answer content, even though rich results are now limited.

## A note on HowTo and others

Google has reduced or removed rich result display for some types, including HowTo. Check Google's current documentation before investing effort in a type solely for a visual feature. The markup can still be harmless and informative, but avoid expecting visible changes in results.

## Do it correctly

Use JSON-LD, mark up only content that is visible to users, keep the data accurate and consistent with your Google Business Profile, and validate with the Rich Results Test and Schema Markup Validator. Review the Enhancements reports in Search Console for errors.

Think of structured data as clear labeling. It makes a well-written page easier to understand, but it works only when the underlying content is already good.

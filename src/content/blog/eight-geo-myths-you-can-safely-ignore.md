Whenever a new channel appears, confident-sounding advice multiplies faster than evidence. Here are claims worth treating with skepticism.

## 1. 'SEO is dead'

Strong SEO remains the base for being retrieved and cited. Many AI features rely on search indexes.

## 2. 'There is a secret prompt or trick'

No hidden formula guarantees mentions. Anything promising guaranteed AI rankings is selling certainty that does not exist.

## 3. 'You need llms.txt to be cited'

Major providers have not confirmed using it. It is optional.

## 4. 'Stuff keywords for the AI'

Keyword stuffing makes content worse for people and unhelpful for models.

## 5. 'AI-generated content at scale will win'

Mass-produced, generic content adds nothing new. Original, expert, specific content is what stands out.

## 6. 'One tool tells you your exact AI ranking'

Answers vary by user, location, session and time. Tools provide samples, not definitive rankings.

## 7. 'You can pay to be recommended'

Organic AI answers are not for sale. Beware of anyone claiming otherwise.

## 8. 'It only matters for big brands'

Specific, local and niche businesses can be strong sources for narrower questions, where large brands are less detailed.

Stay with the fundamentals, test for yourself and be cautious about claims that sound too certain.

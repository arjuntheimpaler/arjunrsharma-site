E-E-A-T stands for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness, concepts from Google's quality guidelines. It is not a single ranking factor, but it describes the qualities that help content be considered reliable. The same qualities help any system, or any person, decide whether to rely on a source.

## Experience

Show that you have actually done what you write about. Include real examples, screenshots, outcomes and lessons from projects. First-hand detail is hard to fake.

## Expertise

Make your credentials clear: qualifications, years in the field, certifications and specialization. Explain topics accurately, at the right depth for the audience.

## Authoritativeness

Authority comes from recognition by others. Build it through citations, links, speaking, publications and consistent presence in your field.

## Trustworthiness

Trust is the foundation. Make it easy to verify who you are and how to contact you, be accurate, disclose relationships, cite sources and be transparent about limits.

## Practical steps

- Put named authors with bios and credentials on every article.
- Link to reputable sources for factual claims.
- Display reviews, case studies, certifications and affiliations.
- Provide a clear contact page, business details and policies.
- Correct errors openly when you find them.

Build these signals into your standard publishing process, not as a one-time cleanup, so every page carries them from the start.

Building a reputation is a long game, but it does not have to feel vague. This roadmap breaks a year into four quarters, each with a clear focus. Adjust the pace to your capacity.

## Quarter 1: Clarify and set up

- Define your positioning: who you help, what outcome you create and how you are different.
- Write your short, medium and long bios, and use them everywhere.
- Update your LinkedIn profile section by section and align your visuals.
- Launch or refresh your website with clear service pages, an About page and contact options.
- Choose three or four content pillars and build an idea bank of at least 30 ideas.
- Start posting two or three times a week on your main platform.

## Quarter 2: Build proof and rhythm

- Publish your first two or three case studies with client permission.
- Collect testimonials and LinkedIn recommendations.
- Start a newsletter or a regular long-form article cadence.
- Add Person and Organization structured data to your site.
- Review what resonates and refine your content mix.

## Quarter 3: Expand reach

- Pitch yourself to three to five podcasts, events or publications that match your audience.
- Write guest articles with proper author bios.
- Create a lead magnet and attach it to a simple email sign-up.
- Build relationships with referral partners and complementary professionals.
- Test what AI tools say about you, and strengthen weak spots.

## Quarter 4: Compound and refine

- Publish a signature piece, such as original research, a detailed guide or a framework.
- Update older content and case studies with new results.
- Review the year: which channels produced enquiries, referrals and speaking invitations.
- Refine your positioning based on the work you most want to do.
- Plan the next year with fewer, stronger initiatives.

## Measure progress

Track a handful of indicators each quarter: qualified enquiries, referral sources, profile visits, newsletter growth, appearances and the quality of inbound leads. Notice shifts in how people describe you when they contact you.

## The principle

A personal brand is built by repeated, consistent proof over time. Clear positioning, honest evidence and steady presence are not glamorous, but they are what makes the right clients choose you.

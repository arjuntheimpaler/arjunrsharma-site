Managing local SEO for one location is manageable. Managing it for five, twenty or a hundred requires structure. Without it, information drifts out of date, reviews go unanswered and pages fall into duplication.

## One profile per real location

Each physical location that meets Google's guidelines should have its own Business Profile with its own address, phone number, hours and local category selections. Do not create profiles for locations without a real presence. Use a local phone number where possible, and keep a master record of every location's details.

## One page per location

Create a unique page for each location on your website, as described in the location pages guidance, and link each profile to its own page. Include team members, local reviews, directions, local photos and unique content. Add a central locations directory that links to all of them.

## Standardize with room for local voice

Create a consistent template for structure and branding, but require local content on every page. Avoid templated text that only swaps the city.

## Set up governance

- Assign an owner for each location's profile, even if managed centrally.
- Use a central tool or spreadsheet to track details, categories, logins and updates.
- Create a process for hours changes, closures, new services and holiday updates.
- Standardize how reviews are requested and responded to, with local personalization.

## Citations and data

Keep each location's name, address and phone details consistent across directories. Use a data aggregator or listing tool if the volume is large.

## Reviews at scale

Track review count, rating and response time per location. Teach each location team to ask for reviews, and highlight top performers. Reply to reviews locally, with names and specifics, not generic corporate responses.

## Reporting

Report by location: profile views, calls, direction requests, review volume, local rankings and conversions. Comparing locations shows which need help and which practices to copy.

## Watch for cannibalization and duplication

Avoid having several pages compete for the same local phrase. Make sure the service pages and location pages have distinct roles, and use internal linking to clarify them.

Multi-location success rests on consistent data, genuine local content and clear ownership. Build those three, and the rest becomes easier to scale.

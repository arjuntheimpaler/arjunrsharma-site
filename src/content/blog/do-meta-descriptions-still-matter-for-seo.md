A meta description is the short summary that can appear under your title in search results. Google has said it does not use meta descriptions as a ranking factor, so changing one will not directly move you up the results. That does not make them pointless.

## Why they still matter

The description is part of the snippet a searcher reads before deciding which result to click. A clear, relevant summary can raise click-through rate, which means more visitors from the same ranking position. It is a conversion lever, not a ranking lever.

## Google may rewrite it

Search engines often replace your description with text pulled from the page that better matches the query. That is normal. Writing a good description still helps because it is used often enough, particularly for your core queries, and it sets a clear intent for the page.

## How to write a good one

- Keep it around 120 to 155 characters so it is less likely to be truncated.
- Summarize what the page offers and who it is for in plain language.
- Include the main topic naturally, since matched words are often shown in bold.
- End with a gentle reason to act, such as 'See pricing and book a call.'
- Make each description unique to its page.

## Where to focus

You do not need to hand-write descriptions for every page on a large site. Prioritize your homepage, service pages, location pages and your best-performing articles. For lower-value pages, a well-structured page often gives search engines enough to build a decent snippet automatically.

Treat meta descriptions as small pieces of advertising copy for your page. They will not fix a poor page, but on the pages that already rank, a better description is a cheap way to win more clicks.

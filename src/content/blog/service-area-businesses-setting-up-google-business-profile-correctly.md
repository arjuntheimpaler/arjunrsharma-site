A service area business, or SAB, serves customers at their location instead of at a shop or office the public visits. Examples include plumbers, cleaners, electricians, mobile mechanics and many consultants who work on site.

## Hide or show the address

If customers do not visit your premises, hide your address and define a service area. Showing a home address publicly is not required and may not be wise. If you do serve customers at your location during set hours, you can show it.

## Define your service area

List the cities, regions or postal codes you actually serve. Keep it realistic. Listing an area very far from your base will not reliably expand your visibility, since distance still matters.

## Avoid common mistakes

- Do not use a virtual office, mailbox or fake address to appear in a city where you have no presence.
- Do not create multiple profiles for the same business to cover more areas.
- Do not stuff the name with service area keywords.

## Support it with your website

Create service pages for what you offer and, where justified, pages for the main areas you serve, with real local detail. Add a clear service area description and consistent contact information.

Stay within the guidelines. Suspensions are common for SABs that bend the rules and can take time to resolve.

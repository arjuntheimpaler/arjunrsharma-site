Internal links are links from one page on your site to another. They cost nothing, you control them completely, and they are one of the most consistently underused tools in SEO. Most small business sites have far fewer than they should.

## What internal links do

They help search engines discover your pages, since crawlers move from link to link. They signal which pages matter most, because pages with many relevant links pointing to them are treated as more important. And their anchor text, the clickable words, helps explain what the destination is about. They also help visitors, guiding them from a helpful article to the service that solves their problem.

## A simple structure to aim for

Think of your site as a hub-and-spoke system. Your main service page is the hub. Supporting articles, case studies and FAQs are the spokes. Each spoke should link to the hub, and the hub should link out to the most useful spokes. Related articles should link to each other where it helps the reader.

## How to add them well

- Use descriptive anchor text such as 'local SEO for dental clinics' rather than 'click here'.
- Link where it is useful to the reader, not where it is convenient for you.
- Link from older, established pages to new content so it gets discovered and gains strength sooner.
- Do not put dozens of links in a single paragraph. A few relevant links are better than many.

## Find your opportunities

Start with your most important service pages. Search your own site for the topic using 'site:yourdomain.com keyword' in Google and look at which articles mention it without linking. Each one is an easy opportunity. Also check for orphan pages, which have no links pointing to them and are hard for search engines to find.

Make internal linking part of your publishing routine: whenever you publish something new, add links to it from at least three relevant existing pages, and link out to relevant pages from the new one. Over a year, that habit quietly strengthens your whole site.

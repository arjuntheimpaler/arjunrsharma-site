Links from relevant local websites signal that your business is a real, respected part of the community. They support both organic rankings and local prominence. The best opportunities usually come from relationships you already have or can build easily.

## Where local links come from

- Chambers of commerce, business associations and industry bodies.
- Local news sites, magazines and blogs, particularly when you have something newsworthy.
- Sponsorships of events, sports teams, charities and community groups.
- Suppliers, partners and complementary businesses that can list or recommend you.
- Local colleges and schools, through talks, internships or scholarships.
- Awards and 'best of' lists run by credible organizations.
- Events you host or speak at, which are usually listed on organizer websites.

## Create something worth linking to

A local resource, such as a neighborhood guide, a local cost guide or a data piece about your market, gives people a reason to link. Press-worthy moments such as an anniversary, a community project or an expansion can earn coverage.

## Keep it natural

Avoid buying links or spamming directories. A handful of relevant local links is more valuable than hundreds of irrelevant ones. Track where your links come from and thank the sources, as relationships often lead to further mentions.

An orphan page is a page on your website that no other page links to. Visitors cannot reach it by clicking around, and search engines have a hard time discovering it or judging how important it is. Orphan pages are surprisingly common, especially on sites that have been redesigned or have grown over several years.

## Why orphan pages happen

They usually appear after a redesign changes the navigation, when old blog posts drop off category pages, when landing pages are created for a campaign and never linked, or when a page is published but forgotten.

## Why they hurt

Search engines mostly find pages by following links. A page with no internal links may never be crawled, or may be crawled rarely and treated as unimportant. Even if it is indexed, it receives no internal authority, so it has little chance of ranking.

## How to find them

- Export all URLs from your sitemap or CMS.
- Crawl your site with an SEO crawler to list every page reachable through links.
- Compare the two lists. URLs in the sitemap but missing from the crawl are likely orphans.
- Check Search Console and analytics for pages that get impressions or visits but are not linked internally.

## How to fix them

Decide what each orphan deserves. If the page is valuable, add links to it from relevant existing pages and include it in navigation or a relevant hub page. If it is outdated, update it first. If it has no value, either delete it with a redirect to the closest relevant page or mark it noindex.

Run an orphan check after every redesign and a couple of times a year. It is a quick job with a good payoff, because the pages often already contain good content that simply needs to be connected to the rest of the site.

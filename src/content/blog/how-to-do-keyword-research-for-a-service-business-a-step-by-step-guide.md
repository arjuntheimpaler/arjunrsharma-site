Keyword research is the process of finding the words and phrases your potential clients type into search engines, then deciding which ones deserve a page on your site. Done well, it stops you writing content nobody searches for and points you at the searches that lead to enquiries.

## Step 1: Start with your services, not a tool

Before opening any software, write down every service you sell and the problems each one solves. Then write down the exact words clients use when they first call you. These are rarely the industry terms you use internally. A client says 'fix my tax mess', not 'tax remediation'. Those natural phrases are the seed of your research.

## Step 2: Mine the sources you already have

You probably hold better keyword data than you realize.

- Sales call and email language: the words prospects use when they describe their problem.
- Google Search Console: queries your site already appears for, including ones you did not target.
- Google's autocomplete and 'People also ask' boxes for your core terms.
- Competitor pages: their headings and navigation show which topics they believe matter.

## Step 3: Expand with a keyword tool

Now use a keyword tool to expand your seed list. Enter your services and note related phrases, questions and variations. Pay attention to the estimated monthly search volume, but treat it as a rough guide. A phrase with 50 searches a month that attracts buyers is worth more than a phrase with 5,000 searches that attracts students and researchers.

## Step 4: Group keywords by intent

Sort every phrase into one of these groups.

- Commercial or transactional: 'hire', 'near me', 'pricing', 'best', 'agency', 'consultant'. These bring buyers and deserve service pages.
- Informational: 'how to', 'what is', 'checklist'. These build trust and suit articles, and they feed the top of your funnel.
- Comparison: 'X vs Y', 'alternatives'. These capture people close to a decision.

## Step 5: Check who currently ranks

Search each priority phrase and look at page one. If it is full of giant publishers and directories, a small service site will struggle. If it contains pages from businesses like yours, the phrase is realistic. Also note what kind of page ranks. If every result is a service page, do not write an article. If every result is a guide, a service page will not compete.

## Step 6: Map keywords to pages

Give each page one primary topic and a small set of closely related phrases. Avoid targeting the same phrase with two pages, since that makes your own pages compete with each other. A simple spreadsheet works: one row per page, with the target phrase, its intent, the current ranking position and the action needed.

## Step 7: Prioritize

Rank your list by three questions. How close is this to revenue? How realistic is it to rank? How much work does the page need? Start with phrases that score well on all three.

Keyword research is not a one-time project. Revisit it every quarter, because your services change, competitors move and the way people phrase searches shifts over time.

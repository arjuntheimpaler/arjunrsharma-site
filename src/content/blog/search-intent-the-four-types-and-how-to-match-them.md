Search intent is the reason behind a search. Two people can type similar words and want completely different things. Search engines try to show whatever type of page best satisfies most people, so if your page does not match the intent, it will struggle to rank no matter how well it is written.

## The four main types

- Informational: the searcher wants to learn something. Examples: 'what is local SEO', 'how to fix slow website'.
- Navigational: the searcher wants a particular site or brand. Examples: 'Google Search Console login'.
- Commercial investigation: the searcher is comparing options. Examples: 'best SEO consultant', 'SEO agency vs freelancer'.
- Transactional: the searcher is ready to act. Examples: 'hire SEO consultant', 'SEO audit price', 'book a consultation'.

## How to read intent

The fastest method is to search the phrase yourself and look at page one. What kinds of pages rank? Guides, service pages, comparison lists, directories, videos? The mix tells you what the search engine believes people want. If the results are all how-to articles, a hard-selling service page will not break in. If they are all service pages, a long blog post is the wrong format.

## Match the format and the depth

Match three things: the page type, the angle and the depth. A 'cost' search wants real price ranges or a clear explanation of what drives price, not a vague 'it depends'. A 'checklist' search wants a list. Study what the best results include, then cover the same ground and add something genuinely useful, such as clearer examples, better structure or first-hand experience.

## Use intent to plan your site

Map your content to the buyer's journey. Informational articles attract people early and build trust. Comparison content helps people choose. Service pages and contact pages capture people ready to buy. Link these together so a reader can move naturally from learning to hiring.

When a page is not ranking, intent mismatch is one of the first things to check. Rewriting a page to fit what searchers actually want often does more than any amount of keyword adjusting.

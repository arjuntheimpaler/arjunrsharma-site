A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Search engines treat links as a form of recommendation, so links from credible, relevant sites help your pages rank. For service businesses, the challenge is earning them without paying for junk or risking penalties.

## Quality beats quantity

A single link from a respected industry publication or a local organization can be worth more than hundreds from low-quality directories. Ask whether the linking site is relevant to your field, genuinely used by real people and unlikely to link to just anyone for a fee. Relevance and trust matter more than raw numbers.

## What not to do

- Buying links or joining link-exchange schemes, which violate search engine guidelines.
- Mass-submitting your site to irrelevant directories.
- Comment and forum spam with links.
- Using private blog networks.

These tactics may produce a short-term bump, but they can lead to manual actions or algorithmic devaluation, and recovering is slow.

## Sustainable ways to earn links

- Create genuinely useful resources: original research from your own client data, free templates, calculators and clear guides that others want to reference.
- Be a source: respond to journalist and expert requests on topics you know well. A mention with a link in a news or industry piece carries weight.
- Contribute guest articles to respected publications in your niche, offering real insight rather than thinly veiled promotion.
- Partner locally: sponsor events, join chambers of commerce, and get listed by associations and suppliers you actually work with.
- Reclaim unlinked mentions: when someone mentions your business without linking, politely ask them to add a link.
- Fix broken links: find broken links on relevant sites and suggest your relevant page as a replacement, only when it truly fits.

## Use your existing relationships

Clients, partners, suppliers and speaking organizers are your easiest sources. A case study that a client is proud to share, or a partner directory listing, often yields a relevant link with a single polite email.

## Measure what matters

Track referring domains over time, not just totals. Look at the relevance of new links and which pages gain them. A slow, steady rise in links from credible, relevant sites is a healthy pattern.

Link building is really relationship building and reputation building. When you publish things worth referencing and show up in your industry, links tend to follow.

Reviews influence both rankings and decisions. Many customers read them before they call, and prominent, recent, detailed reviews are a strong prominence signal. Most satisfied customers will leave a review if asked in the right way at the right time. Few businesses ask consistently.

## Why you need a system

Asking occasionally leads to bursts followed by long gaps. A steady flow of new reviews looks more natural, keeps your profile fresh and gives you steady feedback. Build the request into your workflow so it happens without relying on memory.

## Step 1: Make it easy

Use the direct review link from your Google Business Profile. Place it in emails, text messages, receipts, invoices and a QR code in your premises. The fewer clicks, the more reviews.

## Step 2: Ask at the right moment

Ask when customers are happiest, usually just after a successful outcome, such as when a project is delivered, a treatment is complete or a problem is solved. Waiting a week or more weakens the response.

## Step 3: Ask personally

A personal message from the person who served the customer outperforms a generic blast. Keep it short and specific, and thank them genuinely. Something like: 'It was a pleasure working on your website. If you have a minute, a short review of your experience would help others find us.'

## Step 4: Guide without scripting

You can suggest that customers mention what they used you for and what the experience was like, since that creates useful, specific reviews. Do not write the review for them or tell them what rating to give.

## Step 5: Follow up once

Send one polite reminder a few days later if there is no response. Do not pressure anyone.

## What not to do

- Do not offer discounts, gifts or payments in exchange for reviews. This violates Google's policies.
- Do not 'gate' reviews by asking only happy customers to post publicly while sending unhappy ones to private feedback. This is prohibited.
- Do not buy, fake or swap reviews, or post reviews on behalf of customers.
- Do not post reviews from your office network or ask staff to review.

Violations can lead to review removal or profile suspension, and fake reviews erode trust and may break local consumer laws.

## Step 6: Respond to every review

Thank positive reviewers by name and refer to something specific. For negative reviews, respond calmly, acknowledge the problem, offer to resolve it offline and avoid arguing. Future customers read these responses as evidence of how you treat people.

## Step 7: Use the content

Learn from patterns in the feedback. Share good reviews on your website and social channels, with permission where appropriate. Keywords naturally mentioned by customers, such as services and neighborhoods, help relevance.

## Set a target

Aim for a regular rhythm, such as a handful of new reviews each month, scaled to your volume. Track the count, the average rating and the share with detailed comments. Consistency matters more than any single burst.

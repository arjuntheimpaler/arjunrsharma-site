The title tag is the clickable headline that appears in search results. It tells searchers and search engines what a page is about, and it strongly influences whether someone clicks. A weak title can quietly cost you traffic even when you rank well.

## What a good title does

A good title includes the main topic of the page in natural language, matches what the searcher wants, and gives a reason to click. It is specific, not vague. 'Services' tells nobody anything. 'Local SEO Services for Dental Clinics' tells the right person exactly what they will find.

## Practical rules

- Put the most important words near the start, since the end may be cut off.
- Aim for roughly 50 to 60 characters so the full title is likely to display, though the true limit depends on pixel width.
- Write for people first. Stuffing keywords makes the title unreadable and can look spammy.
- Make every title unique. Duplicate titles confuse search engines and waste opportunities.
- Include your brand name at the end if it helps recognition, but do not let it crowd out the topic.

## Formulas that work

For service pages, try 'Service + Who It Is For + Brand', such as 'SEO Consulting for Law Firms | Arjun R Sharma'. For articles, a clear promise often works well, such as 'How to Write Title Tags That Get Clicks'. Numbers, years and specifics can lift click-through when they are true and relevant.

## Avoid these mistakes

Do not leave titles as 'Home' or the default your website builder generated. Do not repeat the same phrase three times. Do not promise something the page does not deliver, because visitors who bounce straight back tell search engines the page did not satisfy them.

Google sometimes rewrites titles it considers unhelpful, so a clear and accurate title also lowers the chance of that happening. Review your most important pages first, rewrite the weakest titles, and check Search Console in a few weeks to see whether click-through improved.

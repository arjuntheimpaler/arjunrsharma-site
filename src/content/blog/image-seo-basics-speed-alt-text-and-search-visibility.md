Images make pages more engaging, but they are also the most common cause of slow websites. Good image SEO improves speed, accessibility and your chances of appearing in image search.

## Choose and name files sensibly

Use original images where you can, such as real photos of your team, work and office, instead of generic stock. Give files descriptive names like 'dental-clinic-reception-pune.jpg' rather than 'IMG_4821.jpg'. Use hyphens between words.

## Write useful alt text

Alt text describes an image for people who cannot see it, such as screen reader users, and helps search engines understand it. Describe what is in the image in a short, natural sentence. Do not stuff keywords. Decorative images that add nothing can have empty alt text.

## Compress and size correctly

- Resize images to the dimensions they are actually displayed at.
- Compress them to reduce file size without visible loss.
- Use modern formats such as WebP or AVIF where supported.
- Set width and height attributes so the layout does not jump while loading.
- Lazy-load images below the fold, but do not lazy-load your main hero image.

## Support your content

Place images near the text they relate to, and add captions where they help. Include images in your sitemap if they matter for search. Make sure they load for crawlers and are not hidden behind scripts.

Audit your top pages for oversized images first. Fixing a handful of heavy files often produces a noticeable speed gain with very little effort.

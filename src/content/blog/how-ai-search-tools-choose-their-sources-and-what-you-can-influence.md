No one outside the companies that build these systems knows exactly how they select sources, and the details change often. But the general mechanics are understood well enough to guide sensible action. This article explains the broad picture and separates what you can influence from what you cannot.

## Two knowledge pathways

Generative tools draw on two broad kinds of knowledge. The first is what the model learned during training. That knowledge is fixed at a point in time and shapes how the model describes brands, categories and concepts in general. The second is live retrieval. Many tools search the web or an index when answering, fetch relevant pages and use them to ground the response, often with citations.

## Training knowledge

Whether a model knows about your business from training depends on how often and how consistently it appeared in the material it learned from. You cannot edit a model directly, but you can build a durable, consistent presence across credible websites, so that future models are more likely to encounter and represent you accurately.

## Live retrieval

When a tool retrieves information, it usually follows steps like these.

- It rewrites the user's question into one or more search queries.
- It fetches candidate pages from a search index, which might be a major search engine's or its own.
- It selects passages that seem relevant, reliable and clear.
- It composes an answer and often attaches citations.

## What this implies

To be retrieved, your pages must be accessible, indexed and relevant to the queries the tool generates. That is why strong traditional SEO remains valuable: pages that rank well in conventional search are often the ones available for retrieval. Google has said that to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews, a page must be indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet, with no special markup required.

To be selected, the passage must be easy to understand and quote. Specific facts, clear definitions, comparisons and well-structured sections make a passage easier to use than vague marketing copy.

To be trusted, the source should look credible. Signals include clear authorship, accurate information, a consistent identity and corroboration from other reputable sources.

## What you can influence

- Accessibility: allow relevant crawlers and keep pages fast and indexable.
- Content quality: answer questions directly, with specific, verifiable detail.
- Presence: be described consistently and favorably across your site, profiles, directories, reviews and publications.
- Freshness: keep important pages updated.

## What you cannot control

You cannot guarantee a citation, force a model to name you or predict how a particular system will weigh sources next month. Different tools also behave differently from one another, and even the same question can produce different answers on different days.

## A practical stance

Focus on the fundamentals that raise the likelihood of inclusion across all systems, test regularly by asking the questions your buyers ask, and adjust based on what you see. Treat any claim of a secret formula with skepticism.

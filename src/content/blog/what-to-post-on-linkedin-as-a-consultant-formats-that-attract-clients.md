Posting on LinkedIn works best when it helps the right people understand what you know and how you think. You do not need to post daily, or to chase virality. You need consistency and relevance.

## Post types that work

- Practical how-tos: a short set of steps for a problem your clients face.
- Client lessons: what you learned from a recent project, with details anonymized.
- Case study snapshots: a problem, what you did and what happened.
- Opinions: a clear point of view on a common practice, with reasoning.
- Mistakes and corrections: what you got wrong and what you do now.
- Frameworks: a simple model that organizes how you think.
- Answers to client questions.
- Behind the scenes of how you work.

## Writing tips

Open with a clear first line that gives a reason to continue. Keep sentences short and paragraphs brief. Be specific, and use real numbers, examples and details where you can. End with a thought, a question or a simple takeaway. Write the way you speak.

## A realistic rhythm

For most consultants, two or three posts a week is sustainable. Block an hour weekly to draft several posts at once, then schedule them. Spend ten minutes a day replying to comments and engaging thoughtfully with others' posts, which often builds relationships faster than posting.

## Connect posts to conversations

Posts create warm leads. Notice who engages, and start genuine conversations. Avoid sending automated pitches. A helpful reply is far more effective than a template.

## Track what matters

Watch profile visits, connection requests from your target audience, inbound messages and enquiries. These are better indicators of success than the number of reactions.

A site that is easy for AI tools to use has the right pages, each doing a clear job. Here are ten page types to build or strengthen. Together they give retrieval systems accurate, specific material to draw on.

## 1. A clear 'About' page

State who you are, your specialty, your location, your experience and your credentials. Include founder or team bios. This is the anchor for how your business is described.

## 2. Individual service pages

Give each service its own page that defines the service, who it suits, what is included, the process, the timeline and how to get started. Avoid combining everything on one page.

## 3. Pricing or 'cost' guidance

Cost questions are extremely common. Even if you do not publish exact prices, explain typical ranges, what influences cost and what clients get at different levels.

## 4. Comparison pages

Create honest pages comparing approaches, such as 'freelance consultant vs agency' or 'in-house vs outsourced SEO'. Buyers ask these questions, and they naturally invite a recommendation.

## 5. Location pages

For businesses serving specific areas, build pages with real local detail, not duplicated text with the city swapped.

## 6. Case studies

Show the problem, approach, outcome and context. Specific, attributed results are highly citable.

## 7. FAQ pages and sections

Answer the questions buyers ask, directly and concisely.

## 8. Process or 'how we work' page

Explain what happens from first call to delivery. It reduces uncertainty for buyers and gives AI tools clear steps to describe.

## 9. Guides and educational articles

Publish in-depth content on the problems your clients face. Demonstrate expertise and build topical authority.

## 10. Glossary and definitions

Define the key terms in your field in clear language. Glossaries create many extractable passages and strengthen your topical coverage.

## Connect them

Link these pages together logically, keep your business details consistent across them, and use structured data to clarify the business, the services and the content. Review and update them at least twice a year.

Start with the pages that are missing or weakest, usually pricing guidance, comparisons and case studies, and improve from there.
